# Giving your workday a beginning, a spine, and an end

*A calm operating system, built from small AI skills.*

Most of us now work next to an AI all day. It's good at tasks. It's terrible at rhythm. A session starts whenever, sprawls wherever, and ends when you're too tired to notice you should've stopped an hour ago. Ideas you had mid-flow vanish. You can't remember what you decided last Tuesday. There's no opening, no closing — just an open editor and a long blur.

So I spent a day building the missing rhythm: a set of small, composable skills that give a working day shape, plus a quiet gate that helps me start and stop. None of it nags. None of it is clever for its own sake. It's calm technology — it does one thing, then gets out of the way.

Here's what it actually does, by the moment of the day you'd use it.

## You wake up — before the screen

The day shouldn't start at the keyboard. There's a short **wake-up** routine that lives on your phone, not your laptop: light first, water, a few minutes of stillness, a little movement. It's deliberately *off* the computer — the screen is the wrong device for waking up. The laptop's only job comes later, when it gently asks "how did the morning go?" before any work begins. Wellness first, then work.

## You sit down — you name what you're doing

Two tiny declarations at the start of a work session, and the assistant holds them for the whole session:

- **Intention** — *what* you're focused on. ("Ship the export feature.") The assistant keeps the conversation on that thread. When a tangent shows up — and it always does — it doesn't chase it; it quietly files it for later and brings you back.
- **Appetite** — *how deep* you want to go. A quick check, a light look, a full analysis, or a deep dive. Set it once and every answer matches that depth — no firehose when you wanted a sentence, no one-liner when you wanted the works. (The name's a nod to Basecamp's Shape Up, where "appetite" is the time you're *willing* to spend on something — fixed up front, the opposite of an estimate. Same spirit, pointed at depth instead of schedule.)

Two dials, set when you sit down, true for the session. They make the assistant feel like it's working *with your attention* instead of against it.

## You work — and tangents don't derail you

The best ideas arrive at the worst times. A **sidenote** handles them two ways: capture it in a second and keep going, or hand it to a background helper that goes off and explores it *while you stay on your main task*. Either way the thought is safe and your focus is intact. Nothing is lost; nothing derails you. That's the whole trick — you protect focus with structure, not willpower.

Everything you toss aside this way — ideas, bugs, open questions — lands in a private trace you can sweep later.

## You wind down — the day gets an ending

This is the part most tools skip entirely, and it's the one that matters most.

A gentle **gate** notices the evening coming and starts favoring "land what's open" over "start something big." If you push past your own stop time, it firms up — not to punish you, but because *you* asked it to, in the morning, when you were clear-headed. You can always override it; it's your rule, and it bends when you mean it.

Then a **sign-off** turns the close into a small ceremony: a quick look back at the day, a note of what you decided and shipped, and a one-line commitment — *I'm done now*. You sign it, and that signature is what flips the gate fully closed for the night. Optionally it can shut the doors that pull you off (the time-sink sites), until morning. The day ends because you ended it, on purpose — not because you ran out of energy.

## You look back — nothing's lost

Two longer-range moves:

- **Recall** sweeps any window of days and hands you the gist, coarse first — and it knows the difference between what you actually committed to and what was just a passing thought.
- A **weekly review** zooms all the way out: how your habits are trending, what's still open, what shipped. It's a thinking session, not a doing session — building is switched off, on purpose, so you can actually reflect.

## The shape underneath

A few principles hold the whole thing together, and they're the part worth stealing even if you never build this exact set:

- **It asks once, then goes quiet.** A nudge fires a single time and falls silent. Success is the tool disappearing.
- **You're always in control.** Every limit is one you set yourself, and every one bends when you genuinely mean it. Nothing coerces.
- **Depth on demand.** It starts with the big picture and lets you zoom in — never a wall of detail, never hiding it behind a dozen clicks.
- **Capture, don't chase.** A stray thought becomes a saved note, not a derailment.
- **Private by default.** It all lives on your machine. Personal lists never leave it.

None of these skills is big. The point isn't any one of them — it's that together they give the day a beginning, a spine, and an end. The assistant stops being an always-on tab you fall into and becomes something with edges: it opens with you, works with you, and closes with you.

That's the bet: the most useful thing an AI can give a workday isn't more output. It's rhythm.
