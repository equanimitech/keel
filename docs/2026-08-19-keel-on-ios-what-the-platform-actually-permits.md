Exploration notes, not a decision. Nothing here has been committed to.

## Problem

keel's browser surface (`apps/browser`, MV3 via WXT) is a desktop extension. The question was how to reach iOS, and whether the answer is a Safari extension, our own Chromium-based browser as "the kairos app", or something else. Underneath it: how much of keel's observe half survives the trip.

## What the platform permits

### Safari Web Extensions on iOS

Real, since iOS 15. Ships wrapped in a native container app generated by Apple's `safari-web-extension-converter`. The user installs the app, enables the extension in Settings, and grants per-site access.

What ports from our manifest: `declarativeNetRequest` redirect to `block.html`, content scripts, popup, storage. The gate half works.

What breaks:

- The MV3 background is non-persistent and iOS unloads it aggressively. `SFSafariApplication` does not exist on iOS, so the native container cannot wake the background script back up. Continuous observe is not reliable.
- `commands` (the Cmd+Shift+K cooldown) has no iOS equivalent.
- `favicon`, `key`, `incognito: spanning` are Chrome concepts.
- `nativeMessaging` exists on iOS but is pointless with no local daemon.
- `idle` behaves differently.

Good news from the code: `modules/relay/client.ts` is fail-open and `activity/writer.ts` already persists to extension-local IndexedDB with the desktop relay as an optional flush. On iOS the extension observes locally and never syncs. Nothing crashes.

### Our own Chromium browser

Ruled out. You cannot ship Chromium on iOS: non-WebKit engines are EU-only, require BrowserEngineKit, and Apple forces a separate EU-only binary, which is why neither Google nor Mozilla has shipped one. Outside the EU it is WebKit-only. So "our own navigator" on iOS is a WebKit wrapper with Safari's constraints and none of Safari's installed base.

Strategically it is also the wrong altitude. It is a Level 10 move (structure of flows) that trades keel's scarcest asset, distribution, for plumbing. It inverts the kairos kernel's stated design (a contract, deliberately not a package, because the instruments span TypeScript, Rust and plain JS). And a Chromium fork is a permanent upstream-merge tax on a one-person studio.

### Apple's Screen Time stack

The sanctioned path. `FamilyControls` for authorization, `ManagedSettings` with `store.shield.webDomains` for the gate, `DeviceActivity` for scheduled monitoring in an OS-woken extension.

Hard limits:

- **`DeviceActivityReport` cannot export anything.** It is sandboxed with no network, App Group reads succeed but writes are silently dropped, and file writes fail. Apple's forum position: there is no way to pass data from the report extension back to the host app. It renders a SwiftUI view and the data dies there.
- `DeviceActivityMonitor` and `ShieldActionExtension` *can* write to the App Group. Monitor has a 6MB memory ceiling.
- Tokens are opaque by design. You never learn which app or domain, only that a user-picked selection crossed a threshold.
- Tokens are not stable; iOS silently reissues them after OS or app updates.
- `shield.webDomains` has a token count limit (~50 reported).
- The `FamilyControls` entitlement is a request to Apple, typically 4 to 8 weeks, not guaranteed, and needed per extension bundle ID.

### The DNS / network-filter family

The only route to real domain-level visibility on iOS, and effectively closed to us.

- `NEDNSProxyProvider`: managed and supervised devices only.
- `NEFilterDataProvider`: supervised only, with narrow exceptions for per-app managed mode and a Screen Time-tied case.
- Neither functions in a consumer App Store app on an unsupervised iPhone.

Which is why Freedom, AdGuard and NextDNS use a manually-installed DNS configuration profile pointing at a *remote* resolver. That works, and it means running a server that sees every DNS query from every device.

It is also structurally binary as an intervention. No UI surface: sinkholing under HTTPS yields a cert error or a dead page, never our interstitial, so the breath, the dwell gate and the cooldown have nowhere to render. Dwell-blind, since DNS sees a resolution and not time-on-page, and connection reuse plus caching decouple queries from sessions. App-blind, since Instagram-in-Safari and an app hitting the same CDN are indistinguishable. Scheduled blocklists give binary-with-a-calendar, not gradation.

Directly against `modules/relay/client.ts`: shipping zero `host_permissions` is stated there as keel's structural guarantee that it cannot read your browsing. The DNS route is the maximal violation of that, not a tradeoff within it.

## How the category actually handles it

- **Usage numbers come from a counting hack.** Carve the day into ~12 two-hour `DeviceActivityMonitor` intervals (staying under the ~20 monitoring-activity limit), register events at 5-minute thresholds inside each, count `eventDidReachThreshold` callbacks. Each callback is five more minutes. 5-minute granularity, current sessions only, no history, slow to register.
- **The API is unreliable and everyone builds redundancy.** Schedules degrade past ~45 minutes. Callbacks are delayed or dropped under thermal pressure or frequent unlocking. Permissions reset on iOS updates. Blocks fail silently. There is a live bug where `eventDidReachThreshold` fires reporting zero minutes on iOS 26.2. Shipping apps use two or three redundant blocking layers so the stack *feels* solid when the API is not.
- **Frederik Riedel (one sec) has published the rest:** unstable tokens, tokens breaking across `ManagedSettingsStore` instances, no passcode locking so users simply revoke, no API to return from a shield into the app (worked around with local push notifications).
- **Sync is solved by running a server.** Opal, Jomo and Freedom all have accounts, backends and subscriptions.

## Consequences for keel

**iOS keel is gate-strong and observe-weak.** The catch-and-steer half ports. The ambient observe half does not survive in the form the desktop extension has it.

**What can be captured:** threshold crossings from `DeviceActivityMonitor`, and shield interactions from `ShieldActionExtension` (the catch-and-steer moment itself, at the instant it happens). Not activity.

**Granularity is areas, not domains.** The user picks and names a `FamilyActivitySelection`; we never see inside it. Areas are already a first-class kairos kernel concept (`kernel/areas.md`), so the fit is real, but the activity budget is tight: full-day coverage for a *single* selection costs ~12 of the ~20 activities. One aggregate selection sampled well, or several areas sampled coarsely at intervals long enough to hit the 45-minute degradation. Not both.

**Sync is transport, not merge.** The kairos substrate contract is plain files with one writer per collection, and the log is append-only. iOS is just another writer with its own collection; readers union. Volume is dozens of events a day.

Transport options, ranked:

1. Don't, at first. Standalone local log on iOS. Prove the coarse signal is worth something before building a pipe.
2. File drop via a ubiquity container. iOS writes its own append-only file, Apple syncs the folder, a watcher in `apps/tray` copies into `~/.kairos`. Stays inside "plain files", preserves one-writer-per-collection, near-zero code.
3. Bonjour to the tray daemon on the same wifi. Purest local-first, more code, flush-on-foreground regardless.
4. CloudKit private DB. Least work for device-apart sync, but real network egress, and awkward against the extension's own copy: "Always private, on your device."

## Open questions

- Is area-granularity threshold data worth keeping at all, or is iOS keel a gate-only surface with no observe? This decides whether transport is needed.
- If gate-only: does that still serve the productization goal, given the stated identity is observe then catch-and-steer, not walls?
- Does a 5-minute counting approximation on a substrate this flaky clear the bar, when the honest state of the art needs redundant fallbacks to feel solid?

## Next steps

- Nothing committed. Revisit when iOS is actually on the appetite.
- If pursued: file the `FamilyControls` entitlement request first, since 4 to 8 weeks is the long pole and it is not guaranteed.
- Shape the open question above before spending on the entitlement.
