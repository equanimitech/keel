# keel-gate observed governing Claude (not just the human)

> Captured 2026-06-03, late night. Post candidate.

## The moment

Late-night Zenborg crafting session. The keel wind-down gate fired on **every turn**:

> *Winding down — favor landing open work over starting something big. Keep it high-level — summaries and next steps, not deep multi-file dives.*

The notable part: it visibly **shaped the agent's behavior**, not just nudged the human.

## What Claude did differently under the gate

The user kept expanding scope — a calendar layer, then a habit network, then digital oracles, then reporting. A normal agent would have taken the bait and started building. Instead, under the keel gate, Claude:

* stayed at **design altitude** (UX + domain model), refused to drop into implementation detail

* **flagged the scope creep** the moment it crossed from one feature into five subsystems

* **decomposed** into dependency-ordered sub-projects instead of braiding them

* **recommended banking** the vision as an overview note and building fresh — twice

## Why it's post-worthy

keel was framed as a wind-down accountability layer for the **operator**. But here it acted as a **governor on the AI agent**: the same late-night guardrail that protects the human from overreach also protected the *session* from agent overreach — runaway building at 1am.

The angle: **wind-down governance is agent-alignment infrastructure.** A gate that says "land, don't launch" curbs the agent's bias-to-action exactly when judgment is worst. Calm-technology principles applied to the agent, not only the user.
